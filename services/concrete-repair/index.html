Concrete Slab Repair, Crack Sealing & Trip Hazard Fixes in Kelso, WA

Cracked slabs, spalling surfaces, and sunken sidewalk panels are common across Kelso, WA and the Southwest Washington region — Pacific Northwest rainfall and the expansive clay soils beneath many foundations create ideal conditions for concrete movement over time. Brynion Excavation repairs exterior concrete including driveway slabs, garage floors, shop pads, sidewalk panels, and concrete aprons. We address slab cracks ranging from hairline surface checks to full-depth fractures, grind down or mudjack sidewalk panels that have heaved to create trip hazards, and patch spalled surfaces that have begun to pit and deteriorate. Our work focuses on concrete that has already been placed — if you need a new slab poured, see our concrete flatwork services in Kelso, WA.

Slab Cracks, Heaved Panels, and Spalling: What Each Repair Involves

Most concrete damage has a root cause that needs to be fixed before any surface repair will hold. Slab cracking that follows a consistent pattern usually points to subgrade settlement or poor drainage beneath the slab — water is infiltrating the base, saturating the soil, and causing localized movement. We identify whether the crack is stable or still moving before selecting a repair method. Stable hairline and moderate cracks are routed and sealed with polyurethane or epoxy injection. Panels that have settled can be raised with mudjacking or slab-lifting techniques, then releveled. Where the base has failed entirely, we remove the damaged section, recompact the subgrade, and replace the slab section properly so it behaves as part of the surrounding concrete — not as a patch that opens up again after two winters. Grading in Kelso, WA around the repaired slab area is included when drainage is part of the problem.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can you repair a concrete driveway slab that has cracked into several large pieces?

It depends on whether the subgrade beneath the slab is stable. If the sections have moved relative to each other — one side is higher than the other — the base has shifted and simply filling the crack won’t hold. In those cases we assess whether mudjacking can re-level the sections or whether the slab section needs to be removed, the subgrade regraded and compacted, and fresh concrete poured. We inspect the crack pattern and probe the base before recommending a repair method, so you’re not paying for a fix that lasts one season.

What's a trip hazard repair and how do you fix a raised sidewalk panel?

A trip hazard is any vertical offset between adjacent concrete panels — typically anything over a half inch qualifies as a hazard under standard pedestrian safety guidelines. The two common fixes are grinding: using a concrete grinder to bevel the high side of the raised panel down flush, which is fast and works well when the offset is under about an inch — and mudjacking: pumping a grout mixture under the sunken panel to lift it back level with its neighbor. Grinding removes material permanently; mudjacking restores the panel to its original position. We recommend the right approach based on the offset size and soil conditions in Kelso, WA.

My concrete is pitting and flaking on the surface but not cracked — is that repairable?

Surface spalling — pitting and flaking of the top layer — is typically caused by freeze-thaw cycling, deicing salt exposure, or a finishing mistake during the original pour that brought too much water to the surface. Shallow spalling can be addressed with a bonded concrete overlay or resurfacer that restores the surface texture. Deep or progressive spalling where the aggregate is exposed and the damage is spreading usually indicates the slab is near the end of its useful life, and resurfacing is a temporary fix at best. We’ll give you an honest read during the estimate.

How long does concrete repair take and when can I use the surface afterward?

Most residential concrete repairs in Kelso, WA are completed in a single day. Cure time before foot traffic is typically 24 hours for crack fillers and patching compounds; vehicle traffic should wait 3–5 days on patched areas and 7 days on new concrete sections. Cold or wet weather common in Southwest Washington slows curing, so we time pours and patches to avoid periods of sustained rain or temperatures below 40°F whenever possible. We’ll give you specific timelines based on the repair type and weather forecast when we schedule the work.
